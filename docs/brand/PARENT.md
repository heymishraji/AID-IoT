# Inordinate Industrial Innovation — parent brand rules v0 (draft)

_Status: draft v0, 2026-10-27. Structure confirmed in `docs/brand/DISCOVERY.md` and `docs/DECISIONS.md`
(house of brands). Legal details are pending: every `[LEADERSHIP: …]` is a gap, not a fact._

## 1. The structure
- **Inordinate Industrial Innovation Private Limited** is the legal parent.
- **AID IoT** and **AID Defence** are two separate brands with their own visuals, imagery and language.
- The audiences barely overlap. People outside defence should not need to meet AID Defence, and defence
  audiences should not need to meet the IoT business.
- Shared underneath (invisible to most audiences): one font family, one brand umbrella for the identity
  system, shared foundations (grid, spacing, component logic), shared engineering and legal entity.
- Each brand must be liftable, complete, into its own repository (decision log, 2026-10-27).

## 2. The parent's role: legal, not marketing
The parent is a **signature, not a storefront**. It appears where the law, a contract or an investor needs
it, and stays quiet elsewhere. It has no marketing website, no product pages and no social presence by
default. [LEADERSHIP: confirm; decide whether a minimal corporate page is needed.]

Parent identity (minimal): the company name set in the shared font, in a neutral colour. No new logo unless
leadership wants one. [LEADERSHIP: does the parent need a mark?]

## 4. The relationship line (between the brands)
Options considered:
1. **No relationship line (recommended default).** Each brand stands alone; the parent appears only in the
   legal line. Fits the house-of-brands decision and keeps defence and commercial reputations apart.
2. **"A brand of Inordinate Industrial Innovation"** in footers and boilerplates. Honest and neutral; reveals
   the parent but not the sibling. Acceptable where transparency matters (e.g. tenders, About pages).
3. **"An AID company" / endorsed sibling line.** Rejected by default: it links the two brands publicly, which
   the house-of-brands decision exists to avoid. (Research 05 §5.2 proposed it before that decision.)

Note: both names share "AID", so a reader can infer kinship anyway. That is acceptable; the rules below stop
us from *promoting* it. [LEADERSHIP: does "AID" stand for something? Should the shared prefix stay?]

## 5. Careers
- Each brand recruits under its own name for roles that are clearly one-brand.
- Shared roles (finance, HR, shared engineering) recruit under the parent name, with no brand marketing.
- Defence roles may need extra discretion (security clearance, location); job ads follow the AID Defence
  disclosure model (tier 1 only). [LEADERSHIP: confirm process and whether one careers page or two.]

## 6. Rules that keep the brands apart
1. **No cross-linking by default.** No links, logos or "sister company" mentions between the brands on web,
   social, collateral or email.
2. **Separate domains.** One domain per brand. [LEADERSHIP: domains to register; plan to move defence content
   off aid-iot.com, which hosts it today (research 05 §1).]
3. **Separate social accounts** and separate handles; employees choose which brand they represent.
4. **Separate email addresses and signatures** per brand. [LEADERSHIP: separate email domains?]
5. **Separate collateral templates and asset libraries**; each brand package is self-contained.
6. **Separate events:** different stands; staff wear one brand at a time.
7. **Shared products** (OBD is in both portfolios; IIoT Bridge and AI IoT Bridge may be related): each brand
   presents and names its own version in its own voice. No shared product page. [LEADERSHIP: confirm
   which products are shared and their names per brand.]
8. **Customers and proof stay with their brand.** An IoT customer is never cited by AID Defence, and vice versa.
9. **The store and education business** (current site) belongs to neither brand by default.
   [LEADERSHIP: decide its home.]
10. **Exceptions** (e.g. investor material, a joint tender) are approved by the brand owner and recorded in
    `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## 7. What is shared (so consistency does not depend on linking)
One font family; shared spacing, grid and component foundations; shared accessibility standards; shared
governance (one owner, one release process); shared editorial basics (British English, plain language).
Everything else — colour, imagery, voice settings, motion character — belongs to each brand.
