# What makes a great, long-lived logo — research and checklist

_Logo modernisation study, round 1 · 2026-10-30 · written before any variation was drawn, then used to
score them (see `README.md`, "Scorecard")._

This page answers two questions in plain words:

1. What do the people who design long-lived marks say makes a logo work? (§1, turned into a pass/fail
   checklist in §2.)
2. What does each AID touchpoint physically demand of the mark — sizes, stroke widths, gaps, colour
   versions? (§3, turned into the production rules in §4.)

Sources are linked inline and listed in §5. Where sources disagree (common for production figures), the
range is given and the stricter figure is used for AID.

---

## 1. What the experts agree on

### 1.1 A logo identifies; it does not explain
Paul Rand: "A logo doesn't sell (directly), it identifies", and "A logo derives its meaning from the quality
of the thing it symbolizes, not the other way around." Its role is "to point, to designate — in as simple a
manner as possible" ([Rand, *Logos, Flags, and Escutcheons*, 1991](https://paulrand.design/writing/articles/1991-logos-flags-and-escutcheons.html)).
Sagi Haviv (Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv) makes the same point from the other side: the firm "doesn't say too
much on the logo"; National Geographic's yellow frame says nothing literal and is stronger for it
([interview, digitaling](https://www.digitaling.com/articles/277570.html)).

**For AID:** the triangulation story ("three references, one fix"; "sense, connect, decide") is a reason
for the drawing, told in one sentence in the guidelines. The mark must not try to *illustrate* GNSS or IoT.

### 1.2 Appropriate, distinctive and memorable, simple
C&G&H's three tests, repeated by Tom Geismar and Sagi Haviv: a mark must be **appropriate** in feeling,
**distinctive and memorable** enough to persist in the mind (Haviv's "doodle test": can someone draw it from
memory?), and **simple** enough to reproduce at every size
([Haviv, "The 3 rules of good logo design"](https://production-blue.amara.org/videos/Jd0UC4Jk5HxE/info/the-3-rules-of-good-logo-design);
[Print interview with Chermayeff, Geismar, Haviv](https://www.printmag.com/branding-identity-design/marks-men-an-interview-with-ivan-chermayeff-tom-geismar-and-sagi-haviv-of-chermayeff-geisma/)).
Haviv on the tension: designers must "walk the line between simplicity and distinctiveness", creating
"something unique and memorable but simple enough to work as an effective identifier"; and "if the mark is
simple and distinctive enough, it can take on any treatment or filter". Also: "choosing an effective logo is
not about what one likes or dislikes: it's about what works" ([Logo Design Love interview](https://www.logodesignlove.com/sagi-haviv-interview)).

**For AID:** the risk named in research 10 §7.2 is exactly this tension — a triangle is simple but generic.
Distinctiveness must come from a *specific* construction detail (separated nodes and links, the gap rule),
not from added decoration.

### 1.3 Rand's effectiveness criteria
Rand lists what a good logo's effectiveness depends on: distinctiveness, visibility, usability (adaptability),
memorability, universality, durability and timelessness; it must be "reproducible in one color and in
exceedingly small sizes" ([Rand 1991](https://paulrand.design/writing/articles/1991-logos-flags-and-escutcheons.html)).
Later writers turned this into the popular "Paul Rand logo test" with simplicity added as a seventh check
([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/how-good-is-your-logo); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/mystory/the-7-step-paul-rand-logo-test)) —
a secondary interpretation, but a useful scoring frame. Rand also warns that "only after it becomes familiar
does a logo function as intended": a new mark is judged too early if judged by first-sight delight.

### 1.4 Timeless beats fashionable
Rand: "quality, not vintage nor vanity, is the determining factor" ([1991](https://paulrand.design/writing/articles/1991-logos-flags-and-escutcheons.html)).
Haviv: "For a logo to work — and work for a long time — it must be simple" ([LDL](https://www.logodesignlove.com/sagi-haviv-interview)).
Michael Bierut aims for identities that balance "timelessness and flexibility" (MIT Media Lab,
[Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/design/the-new-mit-media-lab-identity-by-pentagram/)).

**For AID:** no gradients, glass, glow, 3D bevels or dot-matrix effects in the master. Trend lives in the
applications (motion, photography), which can be refreshed; the drawing should not need to be.

### 1.5 The mark is the seed of a system, and small digital uses now matter most
Bierut: an effective identity system is "the launch pad", not "the rocket ship" ([Designboom interview](https://www.designboom.com/design/michael-bierut-interview/));
and today an email signature or social avatar matters more than the stationery
([GLG talk](https://glg.com/videos/the-significance-of-logos-and-brand-design)). Pentagram's Mohawk mark is
"a building block in a flexible branding system" that generates "countless patterns based on the mark" by
linking the dots of the M ([Pentagram, Mohawk](https://www.pentagram.com/news/mohawk)); MIT Media Lab's
identity is generated from a grid ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/news/mit-media-lab)).

**For AID:** the owner's brief ("elements of the logo usable as backgrounds, patterns, loaders") is
mainstream best practice, provided the parts come from the mark's real construction (node, link, gap,
60° lattice) rather than from arbitrary decoration.

### 1.6 Small sizes need their own drawing
Apple's guidance: simple icons are "easiest for people to understand and recognize"; fine detail "may be hard
to discern at smaller sizes"; find "one element that captures the app's essence" and express it with "a
minimal number of shapes"; a document icon "can display as small as 16x16 px"
([Apple HIG, App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons);
[Icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icons)).
Practitioner guidance for favicons: draw on the 16-pixel grid rather than shrinking the master; strokes under
about 2 px vanish; check on light and dark browser tabs
([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/200687/favicon.html);
[wildandfreetools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/svg-to-png-favicon-all-sizes/)).

**For AID:** a separate "small master" drawn on a 16 px grid, used automatically at 24 px and below.

### 1.7 Ownability: simple geometry is hard to protect
Trademark offices treat basic geometric figures (circles, squares, triangles) as weak: in the US they often
need proof of acquired distinctiveness ([Norris McLaughlin summary of *In re IGT*](https://norrismclaughlin.com/bwob/?p=522));
the EU General Court (2025) held that "excessively simple" basic figures are generally incapable of conveying
a message consumers remember ([Wolters Kluwer trademark blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/trademark-blog/the-general-court-did-not-jump-on-board-acquired-distinctiveness-is-the-only-way-to-registration-for-simple-geometric-shapes/)).

**For AID:** a plain triangle (filled or outlined) is not ownable. The specific arrangement — three
separate nodes, three separate links, a constant gap — is the distinctive, protectable part. Clearance in
Classes 9, 38, 42 and 13 is still required (research 10 §7.2).

### 1.8 Motion and behaviour
Research 10 (§7.1, rules 4–5) found that the strongest current marks describe a behaviour of the product and
design states before shapes (idle, working, done, alert, with reduced-motion versions). A mark built from
parts that can switch on in sequence animates honestly, without morphing tricks.

## 2. The checklist (used to score every variation)

Each criterion has a test that a reviewer, or an agent, can run. **Pass / partial / fail**; scored 2 / 1 / 0.

| # | Criterion | Test (how to check) | Pass when |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Simple** | Count the distinct shapes and the distinct shape *types*. | ≤ 7 shapes, ≤ 2 shape types (e.g. circle + bar), no internal detail under the minimum gap. |
| C2 | **Memorable** (doodle test) | Show for 5 s, hide, ask someone to draw it. | A colleague redraws the structure (three dots, three bars, gaps) correctly from memory. |
| C3 | **Distinctive** (not a generic triangle) | Place beside Delta, Adidas Performance, Google Drive, Arduino, Airtable-style marks, Mitsubishi, recycling sign, "play" icon at 32 px. | It is not mistaken for any of them; the difference is structural, not colour. |
| C4 | **Appropriate** | Read the story sentence, then look. Does the drawing support it without explaining it? Does it suit both brand stances? | Triangulation and sense–connect–decide are *visible* in the construction; neither brand stance is violated. |
| C5 | **Scales to 16 px** | Render at 16, 24, 32 px on light and dark, 1× and 2×. | Three nodes and the triangle read at 16 px (small master allowed); no gap closes into a blob at 24 px. |
| C6 | **One colour and reversed** | Render in solid black, solid white on black, and as a single-ink knock-out. | No information is lost; nothing depends on colour or a gradient. |
| C7 | **Production-safe** | Check smallest gap and stroke against §4 at the minimum stated physical sizes (etch, embroidery, silkscreen, vinyl). | Smallest feature ≥ the stricter source figure at the stated minimum size. |
| C8 | **Timeless** | Remove the newest-looking trait. Is anything left that only exists because it is fashionable? | No gradient, glass, glow, bevel, dot-matrix or trend-specific curve in the master. |
| C9 | **Construction and optical balance** | Overlay the grid: equal gaps, consistent stroke, centred mass; flip vertically and horizontally to spot drift. | Built on a stated grid; optical corrections are deliberate and documented. |
| C10 | **System potential** | Can a pattern, divider, loader, state glyph and icon frame be made from the mark's own parts? | At least five UI elements derive from the construction with no new shapes. |
| C11 | **Motion / behaviour** | Can it animate by switching its own parts on (no morphing), and does the motion carry the story? | A loader and a success state come from the parts; a static reduced-motion state is designed. |
| C12 | **Ownable** | Is the protectable feature specific (arrangement, gap rule), not just the outline? | Distinctive feature survives one-colour reproduction at 16 px. |
| C13 | **Continuity** (owner's brief) | Show beside the current mark. | A person who knows the current mark recognises it as the same mark, evolved. |
| C14 | **Two-brand fit** | Draw the AID IoT and AID Defence treatments from the same skeleton; apply the covered-logo test (STRATEGY-CONTRAST). | Both treatments are recognisably one skeleton yet feel different (open/warm vs closed/quiet). |

Maximum score 28. A recommendation must score ≥ 24 and must not fail C3, C5, C6 or C13.

## 3. Touchpoints: what each medium demands

Figures are practitioner and vendor guidance, not standards; ranges are shown where sources disagree, and the
AID rule uses the stricter end. **All production files must be re-checked with the actual supplier.**

| Touchpoint | What the medium does to a mark | What the mark needs | Source(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PCB silkscreen** | Ink spreads; minimum line about 0.10–0.18 mm by process; text ≥ 0.8–1.0 mm | Small master; smallest gap ≥ 0.2 mm, stroke ≥ 0.15 mm; one colour (white or black); symbol ≥ 4 mm | [JLC FAQ](https://jlc3dp.com/help/answers/detail/49-Minimum-Silkscreen-text-size); [EEVblog](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/eda/minimum-font-size-for-pcb_s/); [ALLPCB](https://www.allpcb.com/blog/pcb-knowledge/the-ultimate-guide-to-silkscreen-font-sizes-for-pcb-component-identification.html) |
| **Laser etch on anodised aluminium / steel** | Contrast is low (white-grey mark on colour); fine hatching blurs; line width depends on spot size | One colour only; no tints; smallest gap ≥ 0.3 mm (test panel); prefer solid shapes over hairlines | [Trotec](https://www.troteclaser.com/en-gb/helpcenter/materials/material-usage-hints/black-marking-anodised-aluminium); [AMADA](https://amadaweldtech.com/blog/4-tips-for-laser-marking-anodized-aluminum/) |
| **Moulded emboss / deboss on plastic enclosures** | Tooling rounds corners; draft angles; texture swallows detail | Raised (embossed) preferred; feature width ≥ 0.7 mm, depth ~0.3–1.0 mm; gaps ≥ 0.7 mm; symbol only | [AIM Processing](https://www.aimprocessing.com/blog/tips-for-including-lettering-or-logos-on-plastic-injection-molded-parts); [Protolabs draft](https://www.protolabs.com/en-gb/resources/design-tips/improving-part-mouldability-with-draft/); [Xometry](https://xometry.pro/en/topic/minimum-embossed-text-size-for-tpu-molding/) |
| **Pad print / small device labels** | Ink gain on curved or textured parts | One colour; small master below 8 mm; gaps ≥ 0.25 mm | (supplier test required; no reliable public figure found) |
| **Embroidery (apparel, caps)** | Satin stitch needs ≥ 1–1.3 mm lines; thin gaps fill in; small detail is dropped by digitisers | Symbol ≥ 15 mm; stroke ≥ 1.3 mm; gap ≥ 1.0 mm; use the heavier small-master geometry; no thin rings | [Printify](https://help.printify.com/hc/en-us/articles/4483608843921-How-do-I-prepare-my-design-for-embroidery); [Printful](https://www.printful.com/uk/blog/how-to-design-a-logo-for-embroidery); [Blackwater](https://blackwaterstudios.co.uk/?p=8864) |
| **Screen print (apparel, merch)** | Ink spread on fabric; halftones look cheap | One or two spot colours; stroke ≥ 0.5 mm; gap ≥ 0.5 mm | (printer test; same logic as vinyl) |
| **Vinyl decals, vehicle livery** | Weeding small pieces; strokes under ~0.5 mm lift | Each separate part ≥ 3 mm at application size (nodes and links are separate pieces — must be big enough to weed); one colour per film | [Binghamton MakerLab](https://www.binghamton.edu/libraries/about/locations/makerlab/services/vinyl-cutting/); [Signs101](https://www.signs101.com/threads/cutting-very-small-vinyl.68387/); [Arlon](https://help.arlon.com/dealing-with-tiny-text) |
| **Billboards, hoardings, fascia, signage** | Viewed from far; legibility index about 1 inch per 10 ft (≈ 25 mm per 3 m), stricter for moving traffic | Mark height ≥ 25 mm per 3 m of viewing distance (≥ 0.25 m at 30 m); gaps must stay open from distance → use the master, never a hairline variant; reversed version for illuminated signs | [Signs.com](https://www.signs.com/blog/?p=16381); [IJSW legibility review](https://journals.shareok.org/ijsw/article/view/9); [Porlier Outdoor](https://billboardinsider.com/design-tips-by-porlier-outdoor/) |
| **Packaging** | Many inks and stocks; often one-colour flexo on corrugated | One-colour and reversed; clear space protected; works on kraft (mid-tone ground) | (general practice) |
| **Stationery, business cards** | Small but close-up; foil and blind emboss possible | Lockup ≥ 8 mm symbol height; blind emboss uses the master (gaps ≥ 0.5 mm) | (general practice) |
| **App icon, favicon** | 16–32 px on tabs; 1024 px master for stores; system masks round the corners | Dedicated 16 px small master; tile version with safe margin; tested on light and dark tabs | [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/200687/favicon.html) |
| **Social avatars** | Circular crop, 32–400 px, often on busy feeds | Symbol centred inside the circle crop with ≥ 15% margin; reversed tile version | [Bierut, GLG](https://glg.com/videos/the-significance-of-logos-and-brand-design) |
| **Presentation slides, video end cards** | Projected; compressed video smears thin detail and colour edges | Lockup on plain ground; motion reveal that ends on the static mark; no thin hairlines | (general practice) |
| **Trade-show booths** | Huge scale; seen from 2–30 m; lit | Master geometry (looks crafted at size); supergraphic and pattern derived from the mark | [Pentagram Mohawk](https://www.pentagram.com/news/mohawk) |
| **AID Defence: rugged equipment, discreet use** | Low-visibility markings use one subdued colour matched to the surface; optical brighteners make marks glow under night vision | One-colour, low-contrast tone-on-tone version; no white, no fluorescent or optically brightened inks; etched or debossed preferred; symbol only, no "DEFENCE" text on equipment where discretion matters | [Legal Clarity on subdued insignia](https://legalclarity.org/subdued-military-patches-and-insignia-colors-and-placement/); [US Air Force museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Upcoming/Photos/igphoto/2000573560/) |
| **Night mode / dark UI** | Light marks on dark grounds look heavier (irradiation) | Reversed version optically thinned slightly, or the same drawing with links at ≥ 90% weight; tested on near-black | (standard type-design practice) |

## 4. Production rules for AID (derived from §3)

These rules are written in the mark's own units so they hold whatever variant is chosen:

- **N** = node diameter, **W** = link (stroke) width, **G** = gap between a node and a link.
- **Rule P1 — smallest feature.** At any physical size, G must be ≥ 0.2 mm (silkscreen), ≥ 0.3 mm (laser),
  ≥ 0.7 mm (moulding), ≥ 1.0 mm (embroidery), ≥ 3 mm per separate piece (cut vinyl). The README gives each
  variant's minimum physical size for each process from its G/height ratio.
- **Rule P2 — small master.** Below 24 px or 8 mm, use the small master (drawn on 16 px grid, heavier, wider
  gaps). Never shrink the master below that.
- **Rule P3 — one colour first.** Every master exists as a one-colour file. Colour is applied, never built in.
- **Rule P4 — reversed and tone-on-tone.** Every master has a reversed use (light on dark) and a tone-on-tone
  use (Defence equipment); the accent node becomes the same colour as the mark in both.
- **Rule P5 — no partial parts.** Never crop off a node, never join a link to a node, never outline the mark.
- **Rule P6 — clear space and minimum sizes** are given per lockup in the README.

## Next steps
1. **Score every variation against §2** (done in `README.md`) — the recommendation must come from the score,
   not from taste, as Haviv advises.
2. **Run the doodle test (C2) with five colleagues** — it is the one criterion that cannot be checked by
   rendering; it needs people.
3. **Order supplier test panels** (laser etch, pad print, embroidery) using the recommended small master —
   §3 figures are vendor guidance and must be confirmed on AID's real materials before release.
