Logo modernisation · study round 1 · 30 October 2026

Same mark. One story. Built to work everywhere.

We keep the current AID mark — three nodes, three links, a crossbar-less A — and give it the story it was missing: triangulation. A position fix needs at least three references; that is how NavIC works, and three nodes joined by three links is also what AID builds: a device, a connection, a platform.

Every variation below was drawn after a research pass (RESEARCH.md) and scored against a 14-point checklist. The recommendation comes from the score.

SenseBottom-left node: the device in the field.
ConnectBottom-right node: the link that carries the signal.
DecideThe apex: the platform, and the fix. The one node that may carry colour.

Eight variations of the current mark

What changed, what stayed, how each one behaves

Each card shows the mark in one colour on light and dark grounds at 120, 64, 32, 24 and 16 px (24 px and below use the small master drawn on a 16 px grid), then in the six candidate colour routes. Scores are out of 28 against the checklist in RESEARCH.md.

Refined refined

25/28

Same parts, corrected: lighter even links, larger nodes, one constant gap.

In the colour routes (AID IoT on warm light; AID Defence on dark)

What changed
Links are lighter (10 units on the 120 grid, was 14.4) and exactly even; nodes are larger (18 vs 17) so they read as things, not as line ends; one constant gap of 6 everywhere; the base link is drawn 6% thinner because horizontal strokes look heavier than diagonals; the whole mark is nudged up 1 unit because a triangle's mass sits low.
Why it is more modern
More air in the counter and a clear contrast between node and link: the precision of current instrument and interface marks, without any effect.
What it keeps
Everything structural: equilateral skeleton, three separate links, three separate nodes, round caps, the crossbar-less A.
At 16 px
Switches to the small master: side 12 on the 16 px grid, base link pixel-aligned at 2 px, gaps of about 1 px. Three nodes and the triangle still read.
Motion
Nodes light and links draw in travel order; this is the geometry the loader uses.
Not a generic triangle
The triangle is never drawn: it is implied by six separated parts. Delta and Adidas are solid; Google Drive is three overlapping bands; this is nodes and gaps.

Recommendation · one skeleton, two treatments

AID IoT: Beacon. AID Defence: Facet.

Beacon and Facet are one system: the same skeleton, the same three nodes and the same gap rule, with two treatments. Beacon (27/28) keeps the current mark almost exactly, removes the three-colour problem and gives AID IoT one warm signal on its apex. Facet (27/28) is the closed, sharper sibling for AID Defence; its only partial score is continuity, which is right for a brand that must look different. Runner-up: Refined (25/28), if the owner wants no accent colour at all. Relay and Fix are kept as behaviours (loader and 'fix acquired'), not as masters.

AID IoT · open, rounded, warm

Round caps, open gaps, one warm accent on the apex node (Copper shown). Motion flows: a signal travels sense → connect → decide.

AID Defence · closed, sharp, dark

Same nodes on the same skeleton; links cut square to each corner, gaps tighter, one restrained accent (Brass shown). Motion is step-wise, like a briefing.

Lockups · Archivo, outlined

Horizontal, stacked and symbol only

Wordmarks are set in Archivo and converted to outlines inside the kit, so files never depend on an installed font. AID IoT: AID Bold + IoT Regular. AID Defence: AID Bold + DEFENCE SemiBold at 56% size, tracked +10%.

Clear space and minimum sizes

Clear space on every side = 2N (two node diameters, about 38% of the symbol height), shown hatched. Nothing — text, edges, other logos — enters it.

ScreenMaster symbol from 25 px; the small master (16 px grid) from 16 to 24 px; never below 16 px. Horizontal lockup at least 24 px tall; stacked at least 56 px.
PrintSymbol at least 6 mm tall; horizontal lockup at least 6 mm tall; stacked at least 14 mm. Below 8 mm use the small master.
Product markingBeacon: PCB silkscreen 4 mm, laser etch 5 mm, moulded emboss 12 mm. Facet (tighter gaps): 4, 6 and 14 mm. Always the small-master geometry, one colour.
Embroidery and vinylEmbroidery: symbol 16 mm (Facet 20 mm), small-master geometry, gaps at least 1 mm. Cut vinyl: symbol at least 25 mm so each node and link weeds as its own piece.
Signage and billboardsSymbol height at least 25 mm for every 3 m of viewing distance (250 mm at 30 m). Use the master, never a hairline variant.
Clear space2N on every side (N = node diameter, about 19% of the symbol height). For the app tile, the symbol keeps a 17% margin inside the tile.

Motion

The mark switches its own parts on

No morphing: nodes light and links draw in the story's order, sense → connect → decide. AID IoT flows; AID Defence steps. With reduced motion, each piece shows a designed static state.

LoadingLoading

IoT: 2.4 s loop; each node lights, then the link to the next draws. Defence: 3.2 s, parts switch on whole, then the fix. Static state: all nodes on, links at 30%.

Fix acquired

Nodes in, links converge, the fix lands at the centre with a closing ring (1.4 s, plays once). Use for "connected", "position fixed", "saved".

Logo reveal

Video end cards and app launch: nodes appear in travel order, links draw node to node, the wordmark follows (1.7 s).

Usage

Do and don't

Do use one colour firstInk for the structure; the accent only on the apex node, and only one accent hue per brand.
Do use the small masterAt 24 px and below, and on any physical mark under 8 mm. The kit switches automatically.
Do let parts recurNodes as bullets, the base link as a divider, the lattice as pattern — always the real proportions, from the kit.

Next steps

What happens next, and why

  1. Owner picks the direction: Beacon + Facet, or the runner-up — Everything downstream (DESIGN.md tokens, UI previews, Figma mockups) needs one skeleton fixed first.
  2. Run the doodle test with five people, and the covered-logo test — C2 is predicted, not measured; it is the only criterion rendering cannot check.
  3. Choose one colour route per brand — The accent node needs a final hue; the mark is already built to take any of the six.
  4. Make the touchpoint mockups in Figma from applications.md — Seeing the mark on a device, a van and a slide is how the CEO will judge it.
  5. Order supplier test panels and start trademark clearance (Classes 9, 38, 42, 13) — Production minimums are vendor guidance until tested on AID's own materials; simple geometric marks need early clearance.
