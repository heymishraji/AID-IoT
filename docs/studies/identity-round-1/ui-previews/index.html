AID brand identity · round 1 · 30 Oct 2026

What the research teaches, and how it looks on AID screens

Ten lessons drawn from 51 reference brands, then suggestive screens for both brands built with the AID brand library itself: its tokens, components, type roles and light, dark and night modes. Each colour route is a theme on the same screens, so you compare routes, not swatches. Suggestive, not final design.

LESSON 1

Name products by the job they do, not the part number

Best do
Families and pages are named for the outcome; model codes live on the datasheet.
AID does
Five AID IoT families named by job (Fleet & Mobility, Cold Chain, Industrial Connectivity, Connected Machines, Business Software). Product pages lead with a descriptive heading: "Vehicle tracker for fleets that cannot go dark".
See it
IoT landing (families), IoT product page (heading, model code).
From Samsara, Samsara Vehicle Gateway, Disruptive Technologies, Stripe
LESSON 2

Navigate on two axes: what it is, and what it solves

Best do
A Products menu beside an Outcomes or Industries menu, so buyers enter by either door.
AID does
AID IoT navigation: Products · Outcomes · Industries · Signal platform. Industries shown with real places.
See it
IoT landing navigation and industries row.
From Stripe, Samsara, Atlassian Collections
LESSON 3

Show the mechanism, not the atmosphere

Best do
One worked example or numbered figure explains how it works; hardware and software appear in the same frame.
AID does
"Three nodes, one fix": sense, connect, decide, the same story as the logo. Hero images show the device in place with live data beside it.
See it
IoT landing hero and mechanism, product page figures.
From Linear, Tractian, Augury, Framework
LESSON 4

Ask for a conversation, not a checkout

Best do
A human call to action, repeated calmly: talk to a person, request a briefing.
AID does
AID IoT: "Tell us the problem". AID Defence: "Request a private briefing". One primary action per view.
See it
Every landing and product page.
From Blues, Epirus, Saab
LESSON 5

Prove it calmly, with evidence people can take away

Best do
A short credibility strip, downloadable factbooks and datasheets, a trust centre. The weak ones pile on badges and awards.
AID does
A proof strip that stays empty until leadership confirms numbers; datasheets and installation guides as downloads; a Defence trust section on sovereignty.
See it
IoT landing proof strip, product downloads, Defence trust.
From Zen Technologies, DroneShield, Skydio, Saab; avoid Dedrone's badge wall
LESSON 6

Disclose in tiers for sensitive products

Best do
Public key facts, configurations in prose, performance only in a briefing; missions "powered by" one platform.
AID does
AID Defence states what is public, what is on request and what is briefing-only. Missions are "powered by Lighthouse".
See it
Defence landing (tiers, missions), Lighthouse page.
From Anduril Sentry and Counter Intrusion, Saab Gripen
LESSON 7

Restraint is the identity

Best do
One brand colour plus neutrals; colour reserved for meaning; less chrome, softer borders. One colour change can separate whole brands.
AID does
One colour per brand. Status colours (normal, caution, serious, critical) are reserved and never brand-coloured. Colour marks the one action and the data that matters.
See it
Every screen; dashboard and console status chips.
From Polestar, Nothing, Teenage Engineering, Linear, Siemens Healthineers, GOV.UK
LESSON 8

A mark with behaviour, type with a detail

Best do
Marks that move and change state; symbols that work alone; one detail shared between type and symbol.
AID does
The modernised current mark gives a loader, state glyphs and a pattern; the data face's slashed zero and open 4 are the type detail.
See it
Dashboard and console loaders and state glyphs (from the logo work).
From Mozilla, Decathlon, Rivian, Walmart, Lamborghini
LESSON 9

Step out of the category's sameness

Best do
Choose colour and art direction by auditing competitors, then go where they are not.
AID does
AID IoT avoids navy and orange glow (Copper, Indigo, Jamun). AID Defence avoids monochrome grey and black-and-red threat looks (Brass, Glacier, Bone accents).
See it
Compare view: three routes on the same screen.
From Innomotics, Castelion; avoid Anduril's baseline, Geotab's blue, DroneShield's drama
LESSON 10

The parent is a signature, not a storefront

Best do
The holding company governs and signs; the brands do the talking.
AID does
Inordinate Industrial Innovation appears only in legal footers. Neither brand names the other.
See it
Every footer.
From Alphabet, Booking Holdings
What not to copy
  • A pop-up on arrival and catalogue-style device lists (iTriangle).
  • Carousels that hide proof (Zen Technologies).
  • Badge and award walls, yellow-on-black alarm (Dedrone).
  • Weapons as the hero, aggression as the tone (Epirus, Havoc AI).
  • Dot-matrix gimmicks that age fast (Nothing).
  • Navy and orange glow, glowing meshes, stock "engineer with tablet" (IoT category).
  • All-grey sameness, crosshairs, fake HUDs (defence category).
  • Colour-coding every product, which multiplies colours (Atlassian).
