AID brand and design system: what it takes to build

What a complete, rebrand-proof brand system and design system for AID needs, which free systems to build on, and how to package it so any AI agent produces on-brand websites, product screens, decks, datasheets, banners and print.

BrandsAID · AID IoT · AID Defense

01

The short answer

Build one system with three layers of truth: brand strategy in plain text, every visual value as a design token, and templates that only ever reference tokens. Agents read the text, tools read the tokens, and a rebrand edits one folder.

Strategy

Purpose, positioning per brand, messaging house, voice, naming rules. Written as Markdown agents can follow.

Identity

Logo system, colour, type, imagery, iconography, motion, grids, graphic devices. Each rule has a reason and do/don't examples.

Tokens · source

Every value lives here once, in the W3C design-token format (stable since Oct 2025). Brand × mode × density are switches, not copies. This is what makes a rebrand cheap.

Components

AID-owned code built on Base UI primitives, in the shadcn registry format: one set, re-skinned per brand. Patterns borrowed from Astro (defence consoles), Siemens iX and Carbon (IoT).

Templates

Deck, datasheet, brochure, social, roll-up, label. Each is a fixed layout that takes content as JSON; the AI fills the fields and doesn't design the layout.

Agent layer

DESIGN.md per brand, AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md routers, AID agent skills, llms.txt, and later an MCP server. One source, with generated versions for Claude, Cursor, Figma, Canva and others.

Brand architecture

Both reports agree: one family. They disagree on how close the two brands should sit. Colours below follow the mark: orange for the parent, green for IoT, blue for Defense.

softwareLighthousethe software side of the convoy management system: command, tracking, route fencing, SOS
AID IoT expression
  • Full four-colour mark; leads with orange and green
  • Lighter type weights, more air
  • Plants, cities, assets, people using the product
  • Graphic device: signal flow from device to cloud
  • Voice: optimistic, practical, talks about return on investment
  • Proof: case studies, uptime, savings
AID Defense expression
  • Single-colour mark by default; deep neutral ground, one blue accent
  • Tighter, heavier type; monospace for specs and codes
  • Product renders over photos; operators obscured; export-cleared
  • Graphic device: coordinates, bearings, grids (navigation-led)
  • Voice: calm, exact, understated; never glorifies force
  • Proof: trials, certifications, indigenous content, lifecycle support

Shared by both: type families, grid, components, motion easing, functional colours, data-viz logic. Products and solutions get typographic lockups, not their own logos.

03

What a complete brand system holds

Four layers, about 50 items. ★ marks the minimum first release.

Strategy: why and what we say
  • ★ Purpose, mission, values (with "we do / we don't")
  • ★ Positioning: parent + one per sub-brand
  • ★ Brand promise and audiences / personas
  • ★ Messaging house: umbrella, 3–4 pillars, proof points
  • ★ Tone of voice with sliders per sub-brand
  • ★ Naming architecture and trademark process
  • Vision, personality, brand story, elevator pitches, boilerplate
  • Competitive frame; editorial style guide (spelling, units, acronyms)
Identity: how we look and sound
  • ★ Logo system, lockups, sub-brand and co-brand lockups
  • ★ Clear space and minimum sizes (the mark's dot is a natural unit)
  • ★ Logo misuse (8–12 illustrated don'ts)
  • ★ Colour: core, sub-brand, neutrals, functional, data-viz; HEX/RGB/CMYK/Pantone/OKLCH
  • ★ Typography incl. Devanagari, licences
  • ★ Photography and imagery; ★ layout grids per format
  • Icons, illustration and diagrams, 3D renders, data viz, motion, sonic, graphic devices, accessibility
Application: real artefacts
  • ★ Digital: web patterns, product UI, email, favicons, OG images
  • ★ Presentations: master deck, RFP template, investor deck
  • ★ Collateral: datasheet, solution brief, case study, brochure, flyer, whitepaper
  • Social, stationery, events and booths, signage
  • Product: enclosure CMF, nameplates, regulatory marks, packaging, manuals
  • Defence-specific: classification banners, export notices, rugged marking
  • Recruitment, merchandise, co-branding (Make in India, iDEX)
Governance: how it stays right
  • ★ Named owner and a brand council
  • ★ Approval workflow with turnaround times
  • ★ Single source of truth in one versioned repo
  • ★ Semantic versioning and a changelog
  • ★ AI usage rules: who may generate what; human review for defence
  • Asset library with export-control status per asset
  • Training, quarterly audits, trademark register

Study list: IBM Design Language, Mailchimp content style guide, Atlassian, Uber, Shopify, GE (one monogram across three split companies), Siemens, NASA 1975 manual; Anduril, Palantir, Helsing, Shield AI for defence restraint. Source: report 01.

04

What we need from you before designing

Agencies gather these before any identity work. The answers decide architecture, tone and which templates ship first. 34 questions, grouped by who should answer.

Business and ambition CEO, founders
  1. In one sentence, why does AID exist? What would the world miss without it?
  2. Revenue split today and in three years: IoT hardware / IoT software / IoT solutions / defence products / defence solutions.
  3. Is AID Defense a division, a separate legal entity, or planned to become one? Any JV, outside investor or sale plans?
  4. What must the brand make possible in 24 months? (Win a tender, raise a round, export, hire.)
  5. What is AID not? Markets or work you will refuse.
  6. Where does manufacturing happen; is "made in India" central to the story?
Offer and proof product, engineering, sales
  1. Full list of products, platforms, solutions and services, with current names and owners.
  2. For each: problem, for whom, outcome, proof (deployments, numbers, certifications).
  3. Certifications held: ISO 9001, AS9100, ISO 27001, CE, BIS, DGQA/CEMILAC, iDEX, MIL-STD test reports.
  4. Which customers may be named publicly; which only as "a tri-service customer"?
  5. What can only AID claim, and can each claim be proved?
Audiences sales, BD, customers
  1. Who buys, uses, influences and signs, for IoT and for Defense separately?
  2. What do they worry about when choosing a supplier?
  3. How do they find suppliers: tenders/GeM, shows, referrals, integrators, search?
  4. What do customers say about us unprompted? (5–10 quotes per brand.)
  5. Why have we lost deals?
Competition and category BD, marketing
  1. Top five competitors per brand, Indian and global. Who do we want to be compared with?
  2. Which category conventions must we keep to be trusted, and which can we break?
Personality and voice leadership workshop
  1. If AID IoT and AID Defense were people at the same dinner, how would they differ?
  2. Place each on sliders: formal–conversational, technical–plain, reserved–bold, serious–warm, established–challenger.
  3. Three brands you admire and why; three you would hate to resemble.
  4. Words we always or never use.
Identity assets design
  1. Original vector logo files; the meaning of the triangle and its colours; which parts leadership is attached to.
  2. Existing fonts, colours, templates, photo library, with licences.
  3. Which assets have customer recognition and must survive any redesign?
  4. Physical product: enclosure colours, logo placement, label constraints.
Legal, naming and compliance legal, export control
  1. Registered trademarks and applications for AID, AID IoT, AID Defense, NAVIC, Lighthouse, Signal; domains and handles.
  2. Is "AID" cleared in the target classes? It is a common word and a weak standalone mark.
  3. Export-control classification of each defence product (SCOMET; any ITAR/EAR content).
  4. Who signs off public release of defence images and specifications today?
Operations and governance leadership
  1. Who owns the brand day to day? Who arbitrates between IoT and Defense?
  2. Which tools do teams make material in: PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva, Figma? This decides template formats.
  3. Which outside agencies produce material for us?
  4. Appetite and limits for AI-generated content, especially for Defense.

07

Making a rebrand a one-folder change

Components never name a colour or font. They name a role, the role points to a brand value, and the brand value points to a raw value. Change the raw values and everything downstream follows.

// primitive       →  brand role              →  semantic                       →  CSS
color.orange.500  →  color.brand.primary    →  color.action.primary.bg        →  --aid-color-action-primary-bg
// switches, orthogonal (no explosion of combinations)
brand    aid · aid-iot · aid-defense
mode     light · dark · night (low-light / NVG) · high-contrast
density  comfortable · compact (consoles default to compact)
platform output format only: CSS, Tailwind, Figma, PowerPoint theme, print swatches, embedded C/QML, LLM Markdown
// protected: no brand file may override these
color.status.*   color.symbology.*   focus-ring
Colour
  • Author in OKLCH; generate full scales from the three logo colours
  • Gate on WCAG 2.x AA; report APCA alongside
  • Status colours are separate from brand colours: the brand blue and green would otherwise clash with military symbology colours
  • Pantone and CMYK come from a hand-approved table, checked for colour distance
Pipeline
  • Git holds the source; DTCG 2025.10 format
  • Style Dictionary v5 builds every output; trial Terrazzo
  • CI: lint → scale generation → contrast matrix → print check → build → visual regression → versioned release
  • A "fake brand" built in CI proves nothing is hard-coded

Type candidates (open licence, with Devanagari): IBM Plex family; Inter with Noto Sans Devanagari; Mukta or Hind. Source: report 03.

10

Risks the research found

The name NAVIC clashes with ISRO's NavIC

NavIC is the operating name of India's satellite navigation system. A navigation product called NAVIC reads as ISRO-affiliated, risks trademark refusal, and can never outrank ISRO in search. The current site already uses it as a technology prefix ("NavIC CMS"). Options: rename the product line and say "NavIC-enabled" as a feature (preferred), or keep it only after legal clearance.

"Defense" or "Defence"

The house style is British English, and the Indian MoD, DRDO and press use "Defence". The current site URL is /defence. This is baked into every lockup, so decide before logo work.

Defence claims need evidence before the brand repeats them

The current site lists a loitering munition, grenade module and remote weapon station, and claims "seven systems fielded" with MoD and Army, MIL-STD-810G and AIS-140, with no named customers or certificates. Unproven claims are the biggest credibility risk with defence buyers, and may have export-control implications.

Smaller items

"Lighthouse" collides with Google Lighthouse and India's Directorate General of Lighthouses and Lightships, so a descriptor helps (e.g. "Lighthouse Command"). The electronics store and training courses on the current site blur both brands. Astro UXDS is unmaintained, so copy its patterns rather than depend on the package. Reports flagged that NavIC constellation health is disputed in secondary sources, so avoid "sovereign accuracy" claims until checked.

Confidence: competitor visuals, MIL-STD colour tables, some print specs and font licences came from background knowledge, not fetched sources. Each report tags verified vs unverified claims.

11

Decisions only you can make

How close should AID IoT and AID Defense sit?
Report 01 · branded house

"AID IoT" and "AID Defense" are descriptors of one brand. Same mark, type, grid; different colour emphasis, imagery and voice. Cheaper, and IoT credibility lends weight to Defense.

Report 05 · endorsed brand

Shared foundations, but distinct colour, tone and imagery, a separate defence site, and a quiet "an AID company" line. Better if some enterprise customers or investors avoid defence association.

My recommendation: start as a branded house and write down the triggers for moving Defense to endorsed (separate legal entity, JV or foreign partner, customer or ESG pressure). Tokens make that later move cheap.

12

Next steps

  1. Answer the decisions in section 11 and the discovery questions in section 04

    Strategy has to exist before identity choices can be defended to the CEO, and naming is cheap to fix now and expensive once in every asset.

  2. Set up the library skeleton and token schema, with a fake brand in CI

    Every template, component and skill depends on the token names. Freezing them first means later design work goes straight into the single source of truth.

  3. Write strategy, then identity, into DESIGN.md and VOICE.md per brand

    These are the most portable files: every AI tool can read them on day one, before any component code exists.

  4. Prove the pipeline on three real pieces: a NAVIC datasheet, an AID IoT deck slide, a LinkedIn banner

    Three formats across both brands show whether one token change really propagates, and give the CEO finished work to review rather than abstract guidelines.

  5. Then components, registry and MCP server

    These are the largest effort and depend on settled tokens and identity, so they come last.
