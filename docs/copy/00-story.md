# The story the site tells (narrative spine)

Written with the copywriting skill's Human Action Model: **current discomfort → better vision → path to action**, stated in the hero, then substantiated section by section. Every page uses the same spine at its own scale.

## The one-paragraph story
You run physical things — a plant, a fleet, a depot, a base, a warehouse. Those things already produce the truth about your business every second: a temperature, a litre, a location, a weight. Almost none of it reaches you. You find out from a phone call, a paper register, or tomorrow's shift report. AID-IoT puts a small device on what you already run, carries every reading home even where there is no network, and lets Signal — our software — act before anyone has to. We design the hardware, write the software and run the platform ourselves, so there is no vendor gap for the problem to fall into. It is designed and built in India, fielded with the Indian Army, and running in BigBasket's cold-chain.

## The three beats
| Beat | In the reader's words | Where it lives on the homepage |
|---|---|---|
| Discomfort | "I find out too late, from the wrong source." | Hero subhead; "Three ways you find out today" section |
| Vision | "I know what my operation is doing right now, and it acts on its own." | Hero headline; "What changes" section |
| Path | "One team puts the device on, switches on Signal, and stays." | Primary CTA; "One team, sensor to screen"; "How it starts" |

## Why this is a story and not a keyword list
Each section answers the question the previous one raises: *Too late?* → here is how late, in three real scenes. *So what changes?* → four outcomes, each with a real product doing it. *Why you?* → because we own every layer; here is the vendor-gap problem you have lived through. *How does it start?* → three steps with durations. *Prove it.* → numbers and names. *What about…?* → the seven objections, answered. *Then* the ask.

## Rules carried from the copy skills
- Clarity over cleverness; benefits over features; specificity over vagueness; customer language over company language; one idea per section.
- CTA formula: verb + what they get. "Talk to an engineer" (what happens next is stated beside it). Never "Learn more", "Submit", "Get started".
- No fabricated proof. Anything unverified carries a `[CONFIRM]` tag until signed off.
