## 2. Three ways you find out today
*The discomfort, made concrete. Three scenes the three primary buyers recognise. No product names yet — the reader has to think "that's me" first.*

**Section heading:** Three ways you find out today.

- **The phone call.** The furnace drifted at 2 a.m. The operator noticed at 6. You heard at 7, from someone who heard from someone.
- **The register.** Thirty-eight litres of diesel left Depot 4 on a Tuesday. The paper says 195. You will find the gap at month-end, if you find it at all.
- **The silence.** The convoy left the last tower at 14:10. Until it reaches the next one, nobody knows where five vehicles are.

**Closing line:** None of these are people problems. They are signal problems — the data existed; it just never reached you.

## 3. What changes
*The vision, as four outcomes. Each carries one real product so the claim is provable, but the outcome leads.*

**Section heading:** What changes when the signal gets through.

- **You see it as it happens.** Furnaces, drives, scales, meters and tanks report every reading to one screen — including the ones that were never designed to share. *(IIoT Bridge, IoT Edge Lite)*
- **You see it even where there is no network.** On highways, at border depots and inside secure sites, the record still gets home over a private radio mesh — no SIM, no carrier. *(VMS-O, NavIC CMS, TrackNet)*
- **It acts before you have to.** A missing litre is flagged the moment it goes; a conveyor slows before the scale overloads; an engine will not start for a driver who fails the breath test. *(AID-Fuel, AID-Auto, AidFresh)*
- **It scales without a second vendor.** From one device on one machine to a nationwide rollout, manufactured in India by the team that designed it. *(Signal, manufacturing)*

## 4. One team, sensor to screen
*The unique mechanism, told through the reader's past pain (the vendor gap).*

**Section heading:** The reason most IoT projects stall — and why ours don't.

A typical deployment has four vendors: one for the device, one for the connectivity, one for the cloud, one for the app. When the readings stop, each of them points at the other three. You end up with a box on the wall and a login nobody uses.

At AID-IoT the same team designs the board, writes the firmware, builds Signal and runs it. There is no hand-off for the problem to fall into. If something breaks, one phone number answers — and the person who answers helped build it.

**Sidebar (the layers, plainly):** The device → the code inside it → the link → Signal → the app your team reads. All ours. All built in India.
