# 07 · IoT competitors and design-led exemplars

Researched 27 October 2026 for AID IoT (Inordinate Industrial Innovation Pvt Ltd, New Delhi), a rebrand from scratch.
Personality brief: an empathetic solver that understands the problem first, then delivers a clear, customisable solution. Admires Apple, Siemens and Samsung (clarity for non-technical people). Dislikes AI slop, noise, jargon, too many colours, inconsistency.

## 0. Method and honesty notes

- **[fetched]** = I loaded the live page with WebFetch on 27 Oct 2026 and quote it. **[search]** = verified through a web search result only. **[background]** = my prior knowledge, not verified today; treat as a hypothesis.
- WebFetch returns page *text*, not rendered pixels. So colour, type and motion are **[background]** or inferred from alt text unless stated. That is why section 7 (the Figma board list) exists: capture these pages visually before any look-and-feel decision.
- Not fetchable (blocked, 404 or empty): Zenatix (403), Ather homepage (403; used its `llms.txt` instead), Teltonika (page returned only a title; used search), Rivian (title only), Zepto (empty), Jio IoT (404), Blues `/notecard/` and Particle `/platform/` sub-pages (404).
- Scores are my judgement, 1 to 5. Clarity = could a non-technical buyer say what it is and why they want it within 10 seconds. Distinctiveness = would you recognise the brand with the logo covered.

### 3.1 India: direct competitors and adjacent

**iTriangle Infotech** · https://www.itriangle.net/ [fetched]
- Positioning: "Telematics, The iTriangle Way" / "Smart Tracking. Real-Time Insights. Reliable Performance." Self-described "India's leading provider of Telematics and IoT solutions."
- Audience: fleet operators, auto OEMs, ride-hailing, EV, government (claims empanelment "across all states").
- Look: capture needed. Tone is corporate, uppercase CTAs.
- Explains: device tiers named BASIC / ADVANCE / OEM / EV / ASSET / DASHCAM; model names like "Bharat101 Plus 4G", "TS101Plus 4G", "FleetNex VX101". Platform is "aQuila".
- Organises: header is HOME / ABOUT US / PRODUCTS & SOLUTIONS / SUPPORT HUB / CONTACT US; a deep "Support Hub" of tools (Device Configuration Tool, Ticketing Tool, Config 360 v2, OEM Tool, Debugging Tool).
- Good: the support-tool hub is a real differentiator for OEM/installer audiences; "Bharat101" signals AIS-140/IRNSS compliance.
- Avoid: alphanumeric model soup (TS101, UX101-AL PLUS PLUS); nothing helps a first-time buyer choose.

**Mappls (MapmyIndia)** · https://www.mappls.com [fetched]
- Positioning: "Mappls - Super Map App for Maps, Navigation, Tracking, Safety and more." Swadeshi/Bharat identity.
- Audience: Indian drivers, businesses, map contributors. Hardware ("Mappls Gadgets: Dashcam, HUD, Vehicle Trackers, Infotainments") sits under "Order Online".
- Explains: not at all for IoT; it is a shop link.
- Good: genuine India-first trust, bilingual UI, "Mappls Pin" (a 6-character digital address) as a memorable proprietary idea.
- Avoid: burying hardware inside a consumer app. Lesson in what *not* to do with a multi-product company.

**LocoNav** · https://www.loconav.com [fetched]
- Positioning: "The Future of Fleet Operations Management is Here" / "Single platform to track, monitor, and manage your vehicles, assets, fuel, expenses and more."
- Audience: fleets across 25+ industries; claims 100,000+ customers, 50+ countries.
- Look: capture needed; bold headings, short feature blurbs.
- Explains: one-line per product: Fleet Management, Video Telematics ("AI-based cameras…"), Fuel Monitoring, EV Management System, E-Lock, "EagleAI™ a virtual fleet manager".
- Organises: Products, Solutions, Resources, Company, plus *Supported Hardware* and *APIs for Developers* as top-level items.
- Good: "Supported Hardware" in the top nav treats hardware as a first-class citizen; product names are plain nouns.
- Avoid: "World's Fastest Growing Fleet-Tech Company" style superlatives.

**Fleetx** · https://www.fleetx.ai/ [fetched]
- Positioning: "The Intelligence Layer For The Physical Operations." Five products: TMS, Video & Camera, Fleet Visibility, Fuel Monitoring, Transport ERP; "Five products. One AI Engine."
- Audience: Fortune 500 fleet/logistics, Indian fleet operators.
- Explains: each product has a verb-first sentence: "See fuel loss before it impacts operations."; "Run your entire business from a single screen."
- Good: product line as five short *jobs*; a Gurugram ERP-plus-IoT combination is the closest analogue to AID IoT's own mix (AID ERP plus hardware).
- Avoid: "AI Engine"/"MCP" banners everywhere: category cliché.

**Trak N Tell** · https://www.trakntell.com [fetched]
- Positioning: "Experience India's 1st OEM GPS Vehicle Tracker" / "Track your vehicle anywhere anytime"; "Trusted by 500,000 Indians."
- Audience: individual car/bike owners, families (kids, elderly), fleets, enterprise (L&T, ONGC, Indian Railways).
- Organises: HOME / SHOP / TELEMATICS / ANDROID STEREO / CONTACT; VEHICLE (car, bike, fleet, eBike) and PERSONAL (items, kids & elderly).
- Good: "Vehicle vs Personal" split is by *who the person is protecting*, not by device type. WhatsApp support and EMI are India-native conveniences.
- Avoid: a shop that mixes B2C and enterprise without a clear front door.

**Watsoo** · https://www.watsoo.com [fetched]

## 4. Patterns that work (adapt, do not copy)

1. **Three entry doors:** Samsara and Geotab organise by product, by need and by industry. A buyer who does not know product names can still start from the problem. (Tagbox's "Problems we solve" is the Indian version.)
2. **Outcome verb lines for every product:** Fleetx ("See fuel loss before it impacts operations."), Motive, Samsara. Product name = noun, description = job done.
3. **Hardware explained by installation:** Geotab (plugs into OBD II), Disruptive ("peel-and-stick… take seconds to install"), Blues ("a few lines of JSON"). One physical image per device.
4. **Pricing and humans visible:** Samsara has Pricing in the top nav; Blues has "Talk to a human"; Blues states "no subscriptions or contracts".
5. **Honest maturity labels:** Particle's NEW / BETA / COMING SOON.
6. **Same micro-structure per product:** Framework's Specs · Parts · Downloads · Configure. Intercepts the "inconsistency" dislike.
7. **A single worked example carries the page:** Linear. Also Tagbox's sensing → monitoring → insight chain, Intangles' "predict days before".
8. **Contrarian opening that shows empathy:** Verdigris ("Green dashboards are the most expensive color in the building"), Augury ("Your production isn't the problem. Your tech is.").
9. **Local voice:** Ecozen's "Batti Gul, Fir bhi Cool", Mappls bilingual UI, Trak N Tell WhatsApp support. Rare and memorable in B2B.
10. **Ownership programmes around hardware:** Ather's Eight70 warranty, Assured Buyback.

## 5. Clichés the category is stuck in

- "AI" or "AI agents" in the first line (Motive, Augury, Fleetx, Tuya, Tractian, Siemens, Hex): the single most common opening in 2026. Treat as noise.
- Triplet slogans: Safety/Productivity/Profitability (Motive), Simple/Smart/Secure (Tata), Build/Connect/Manage (Digi), Reliable/Secure/Simple (Disruptive).
- "One platform" (Geotab, Motive, Samsara, Hologram, Fleetx "One AI Engine").
- Mega-menus with 30+ items; footers that disagree with menus (Samsara).
- Stat walls: 100,000+ customers, 99.99% uptime, 45 million devices.
- "Intelligent / WISE / Smart / Digital Twin / Fabric" prefix naming.
- Dark dashboard hero screenshot, glowing map pin, "HUD" aesthetic (consistent with the project rule: navy plus orange glow plus device on sweep plus bento).
- Superlative self-claims: "India's 1st", "World's Fastest Growing", "India's Most Deployed".
- Alphanumeric device names (TS101Plus, FMB965, ALTA XL) with no plain description.
- Catalogue-first sites for OT audiences (Advantech, Moxa, Teltonika).

## 6. Whitespace for AID IoT

1. **No Indian IoT brand explains itself to a non-technical decision-maker.** Incumbents (iTriangle, Watsoo, Trak N Tell) are catalogue or compliance led; Tagbox and Ecozen are the only problem-led ones and look dated or niche.
2. **"Understand the problem first" is under-used.** Only Verdigris and Augury open with the buyer's pain; nobody in India does. AID's page order: *your problem* → *what we saw* → *the solution you can adjust*.
3. **"Customisable" is unclaimed.** Monnit and Blues sell custom development in a menu; Framework owns it in consumer hardware. No telematics firm tells a *configure-it* story ("Configure now").
4. **Hardware + firmware + software + manufacturing under one roof is unusual.** Samsara/Motive are hardware-light narratives; Particle/Blues are infrastructure. A "we make it in India, in our own factory" proof (PCB milling, vending machine, ultrasonic cleaner) is a believable differentiator, if the brand architecture does not look like Watsoo.
5. **A quiet, one-accent, benefit-led brand in a category of dark HUDs.** Nothing proved a restrained system can be iconic; nobody in IoT has taken it.
6. **Sector-adjacent products are an asset if framed right:** cold chain, vehicles, factories as three "rooms" of one home (Ather's "stack" logic), not a catch-all.

## 7. Ten nuances to borrow and make our own

| # | Nuance | From | How AID IoT makes it its own |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problem-first navigation**: a top-level "Problems we solve" entry beside Products | Tagbox, Samsara solutions-by-need | Each problem page = a plain sentence the customer would say ("Diesel keeps disappearing") leading to the product that fixes it |
| 2 | **One worked example per product page**, followed end to end | Linear (DRV-8852), Intangles | e.g. one truck, one trip, one fuel drop, traced from sensor → IIoT Bridge → dashboard alert → action |
| 3 | **Name devices by what they do**, model number one level down | Disruptive Technologies | "AID Fresh" not "TS-7B"; spec table below the fold |
| 4 | **"Peel-and-stick"-style install image** for every device | Disruptive, Geotab | A single sentence plus a photo: "Clip to the OBD port. Two minutes." |
| 5 | **Identical product-page skeleton** (What it solves · How it fits · Specs · Customise · Parts/Support) | Framework | Guarantees consistency; "Customise" slot is where AID's promise lives |
| 6 | **"Clarity, not noise"** as a stated rule for alerts and for the site | Augury | Make it a product principle: fewest alerts, each with the next action. Say it in the brand voice, not as a tagline |
| 7 | **"Talk to a human" and visible pricing/path to pricing** | Blues, Samsara | "Talk to an engineer in Delhi" with a named person/team; ranges rather than hidden quotes |
| 8 | **Honest maturity badges** (Available · Pilot · Coming) | Particle | Lets a house with many products show breadth without over-claiming; ideal for names that "may change" |
| 9 | **Local voice used once, well** | Ecozen | One Hindi/Hinglish phrase as a memorable anchor in a campaign line, not a bilingual site gimmick |
| 10 | **Hardware wrapped in ownership guarantees** | Ather (Eight70, Assured Buyback) | Warranty, firmware-update promise, repairability and "we build it in India" proof, shown as a single "Owner's promise" strip |

(Runner-up: Ultraviolette's "6 levels of safety" number-plus-list pattern for explaining robustness; Framework's footnoted claims for trust.)

## 8. Implications for architecture (feeds the brand/site decision, not a decision itself)

- Treat the multi-product problem as a **three-room** model (move / keep / make: vehicles, cold chain and fresh, factories) with one shared platform story, rather than a 12-item product menu (Advantech/Watsoo trap).
- Keep the name set small and nouns-plain; model numbers in specs only.
- Resist "AI" in the hero until a real feature backs it.
