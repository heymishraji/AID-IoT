# AID Defence — voice guide v0 (draft, agent-ready)

_Status: draft v0, 2026-10-27. Based on the owner's description in `docs/brand/DISCOVERY.md` and
`STRATEGY.md` in this folder. Vocabulary and terminology await leadership._

**For agents:** read this whole file and the disclosure model (`STRATEGY.md` §8) before writing AID Defence
copy. Never invent customers, units, programmes, numbers, certifications or claims. If a fact is missing,
write `[LEADERSHIP: …]`. If you are unsure whether something is sensitive, leave it out.

## 1. Voice in one line
**A senior engineer briefing a senior officer: calm, exact, brief, and respectful of their time.**

## 2. Traits — do and don't

| Trait | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Mature** | Use a steady, measured register. Let facts carry weight. | Exclaim. Use startup slang. Compare ourselves loudly with others. |
| **Clear** | One idea per sentence. Problem, then approach, then outcome. Plain words a senior officer reads in one pass. | Write acronym chains. Lead with specifications. Assume technical background. |
| **Discreet** | Give a glimpse of the problem and approach, then offer a private briefing. | Publish performance, users, locations or architecture. Hint at secrets for effect. |
| **Resolute** | Commit plainly: "We will take this on." State what we will do and by when. | Boast. Use force or combat imagery. Overpromise. |

## 3. Tone sliders
Scale 1–5. The owner set: formal, conversational, reserved and bold, established challenger.

| Slider | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Position | In practice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Casual | formal | ● | | | casual | **2** | Formal yet conversational: full sentences, courteous, no slang; contractions sparingly. |
| Technical ↔ Plain | technical | | | ● | plain | **4** | Plain for the decision-maker. Precision lives in tier 2–3 documents. |
| Reserved ↔ Bold | reserved | | ● | | bold | **2 / 4** | Reserved in what we disclose and how loudly; bold in what we commit to. |
| Serious ↔ Warm | serious | ● | | | warm | **2** | Serious and courteous. Warmth shows as respect, not friendliness. |
| Established ↔ Challenger | established | | ● | | challenger | **3** | Sounds established (calm, structured); acts as a challenger (takes on any requirement). |

## 4. Tone by context
- **Website (tier 1):** shortest, calmest. Problem → approach → invitation to a private briefing.
- **Datasheet and capability statement (tier 2):** plain summary of 2–3 sentences, then bounded specifications.
- **Briefing deck (tier 3):** structured: situation, problem, approach, evidence, what we ask.
- **Software UI (e.g. Lighthouse):** terse, unambiguous, consistent; status words fixed in a glossary.
- **Email replies:** prompt, polite, short; confirm the next step and the channel.
- **Social:** company news and considered thinking. No product specs. No deployment stories.

## 5. Writing rules
1. **Spelling:** British English, and always **"Defence"**, never "Defense", including in headings and URLs.
2. **Sentence length:** aim for 10–16 words; hard limit 22. Paragraphs of 1–3 sentences.
3. **Problem first:** describe capability by the problem it solves. "Commanders see where every vehicle in a
   convoy is", not "5-second NavIC/GNSS tracking with route fencing".
4. **Jargon policy:** plain words first; technical term after, or in tier 2–3 documents. Expand every acronym
   on first use. If a senior non-technical officer would stop to decode it, rewrite it.
5. **Claims only with evidence:** every claim must have a source we can show at the right tier. No evidence,
   no claim. No superlatives ("most advanced", "world-class", "unmatched").
6. **No hype:** no exclamation marks, no "revolutionary", "game-changing", "next-generation".
7. **Never glorify force or violence.** No combat drama, no "lethal", "kill", "dominate", "destroy" as selling
   words. Describe protection, awareness, coordination, reliability.
8. **Disclosure:** check every sentence against the tier (STRATEGY §8). When in doubt, move it down a tier.
9. **Close with an invitation:** most public copy ends with a quiet offer of a private briefing.
10. **Never mention the parent company's other brand**, its customers, the store or education business.

## 8. Before and after
All proof is a placeholder until verified and cleared for the tier.

**1. Product description (NAVIC CMS, website, tier 1)**
- Before: "Our battle-ready NavIC CMS delivers 5-second real-time tracking, ±0.6 m accuracy, AES-256-GCM
  encryption and SatCom failover for next-generation convoy dominance."
- After: "A convoy commander needs to know where every vehicle is, and to reach every one of them. Our convoy
  management system shows the whole convoy on one screen and keeps it in contact. Request a private briefing."
  [LEADERSHIP: verify both capabilities before use.]

**2. Error message (Lighthouse UI)**
- Before: "FATAL: Node comms failure!!! Retry?"
- After: "Vehicle 07 — no position for 4 min. Last known position shown. Retrying automatically."

**3. LinkedIn post**
- Before: "Proud to unveil our deadly new anti-drone system that will crush any aerial threat! 🇮🇳🔥"
- After: "Small drones have changed what protecting a site means. We've been working on how to detect them
  early and respond proportionately. We'll be at [LEADERSHIP: event, if cleared]. Briefings by appointment."

**4. Datasheet intro (tier 2)**
- Before: "The AID ADS is a world-class, multi-layered, AI-enabled counter-UAS platform."
- After: "AID ADS helps protect a site from unauthorised drones. It detects them and supports the operator's
  response. This sheet gives a summary of capability. Full specifications are available in a briefing.
  [LEADERSHIP: approved capability summary and specification set.]"

**5. Call to action**
- Before: "Get a quote" / "Buy now"
- After: "Request a private briefing" (primary) · "Ask for the capability statement" (secondary)

**6. Reply to an enquiry email**
- Before: "Hi! Thanks for reaching out!! Attaching all our product brochures and specs. Let's jump on a call?"
- After: "Thank you for your enquiry. We would be glad to brief you on how we approach convoy management.
  Could you confirm your organisation and a suitable time? We will share detailed material in that meeting.
  Regards, [Name], AID Defence"

## 9. Self-check for agents (answer yes to all)
1. Could a senior non-technical officer understand it in one pass?
2. Does it describe capability by the problem solved, not by specifications?
3. Is every sentence 22 words or fewer, with acronyms expanded on first use?
4. Is every claim backed by evidence we can show — or marked `[LEADERSHIP: …]`?
5. Is it free of hype, superlatives and exclamation marks?
6. Is it free of language or imagery that glorifies force or violence?
7. Is everything in it allowed at this tier (public / on request / briefing only)?
8. Are customers, units, locations and deployments absent unless cleared?
9. Is "Defence" spelled correctly, and terminology consistent with section 6?
10. Does it end with a quiet invitation (usually a private briefing)?
11. No mention of the parent company's other brand, its customers or its products?
