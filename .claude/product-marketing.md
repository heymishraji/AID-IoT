# Product marketing context — AID-IoT (read by the copywriting / site-architecture / cro skills)

## What we sell
End-to-end connected operations for companies that run physical things. We design the device that goes on the machine, vehicle or tank; write the firmware inside it; carry the data over any link (4G, Wi-Fi, satellite, or a private radio mesh with no SIM); run the software platform — **Signal** — in our cloud or on the customer's servers; and manufacture in India. Hardware groups: gateways & edge (IIoT Bridge, Ethernet Bridge, IoT Edge Lite), fleet & fuel (AID-Fuel, AID-Fuel-Marine, VMS-O, VFMS-O, AID-Auto), tracking & convoy (TrackNet, NavIC CMS), freshness & retail (AidFresh, vending controller), defence systems. Software: Signal platform, fleet & fuel apps (FSMS, DEPOT, RAVEN, QUEST), AID-ERP, TraceCraft. Services: product development, PCB design, manufacturing (25+ processes), training.

## Who buys (in priority order)
1. Executive buyer — CEO / COO / plant or depot head; not from IT. Finds out about problems from phone calls, paper registers and next-morning shift reports. Wants: fewer surprises, fewer losses, one accountable partner.
2. Operations owner — fleet manager, warehouse head, production manager. Wants: works with what I have, works offline, my team can use it.
3. Defence / government programme & procurement. Wants: indigenous, fielded, secure (nothing leaves the perimeter), a real contact.
4. Technical evaluator — CTO / OT engineer / integrator. Wants: protocols, APIs, hosting options, datasheets.
5. Builder client (OEM/founder) · educator · store shopper · existing customer · talent.

## Their words (voice of customer, from sales conversations and the current site)
"We only find out at month-end." "The diesel just disappears." "Our PLCs don't talk to anything." "There's no network at the depot / on the highway / at the border." "The last vendor did the hardware and disappeared when the app broke." "Can we host it ourselves?" "Is it Indian?" "Who do I call when it breaks?"

## Differentiator (unique mechanism)
The same team designs the board, writes the firmware, builds the platform and runs it. A typical IoT deployment has four vendors and three hand-offs; ours has one. Data stays in the customer's perimeter if they want it to. Indian-designed and manufactured; fielded with the Indian Army; partnered with IIT Delhi and IIT Jammu; running BigBasket's cold-chain freshness.

## Proof available (permissions pending)
Ministry of Defence, Indian Army, IIT Delhi, IIT Jammu, BigBasket, KHub, JetOne, Precision. Numbers on record: 5,000+ SKUs at zero stock variance (AID-ERP); −12.4% fuel burn (AID-Fuel-Marine voyage); 0 SIMs for fleet tracking (VMS-O); 25+ manufacturing processes; offices in Delhi and Acton, Ontario.

## Objections to handle
Works with my existing machines? · What if there's no network? · Where does my data live? · Who supports it after install? · How long to a pilot? · Is it really Indian / indigenous? · Is the software as serious as the hardware?

## Voice
Confident, plain, specific, calm. The engineer who built it explaining it to a client they respect. British English, sentence case. No exclamation marks, no "revolutionary/seamless/empower/leverage", no jokes in defence contexts. Define technical terms once (e.g. "NavIC — India's own satellite positioning system"). Product code names always carry a plain descriptor.

## Primary action
Talk to an engineer (30-minute call, no sales deck; reply within one business day). Secondary: download a datasheet; see a solution page.
