## 8. Disclosure model
The brand says little in public on purpose. Public material shows **a glimpse of the problem and our
approach**, then invites a private conversation. Three tiers:

| Tier | Who sees it | What it contains | What it never contains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Public** | Anyone | Brand, purpose, problem areas we work on, our approach, product names and one-line roles (if cleared), company facts, cleared certifications, how to request a briefing | Performance figures, architecture, interfaces, frequencies, customer or unit names, deployment locations, real-kit photos |
| **2. On request** | Verified official or partner enquiries | Capability statement, summary datasheets with bounded specifications, compliance documents, cleared case summaries | Sensitive performance data, source detail, unreleased programmes |
| **3. Briefing only** | Invited audience, in person or on a secure channel [LEADERSHIP: NDA / protocol] | Full specifications, demonstrations, architecture, trial evidence, pricing, deployment specifics | Anything beyond the audience's clearance or need to know |

## 9. Brand personality
The owner's words: mature, very clear, no nonsense, on point; private and discreet; formal yet
conversational; reserved but bold; an established challenger that takes on any requirement.

| Trait | What it means | This | Not that |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mature** | Calm, measured, sure of itself. Respects the reader's rank and time. | Composed, courteous, steady | Excitable, startup swagger, chest-beating |
| **Clear** | A senior non-technical officer understands it in one pass. | Plain, exact, on point | Jargon walls, acronym soup, vague |
| **Discreet** | Shows a glimpse, invites a private briefing. Never leads with anything sensitive. | Selective, trustworthy, quiet | Secretive for effect, evasive, coy |
| **Resolute** | An established challenger: takes on any requirement and sees it through. | Bold in commitment, confident, direct | Bold in volume, aggressive, militaristic |

Archetype (optional): **the Sage-Guardian** — knows, explains, protects. Not the Hero or the Warrior.

## 12. What AID Defence never does
- Glorifies force, violence or combat, in words or images.
- Publishes performance, deployment, customer or unit detail outside its tier.
- Claims anything without evidence it can show.
- Mentions or links to the parent company's other brand, the store or education business.
- Uses defence clichés: camouflage, crosshairs, black-and-red threat looks, fake HUDs, tricolour wash (research 05 §5.3).
